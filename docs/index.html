In the emergency room, the wait itself can be dangerous.

People wait in the emergency department to be seen, and often again for a hospital bed. The research says the waiting itself carries risk. Here is how big that problem is.

Talk with us See the numbers

Emergency rooms are where millions of people start their care.

In the United States alone, emergency departments handled 155 million visits in 2022. Each one is a person who came in needing help.

Triage is a quick snapshot, and the standard scale misses people.

At the door, patients are sorted quickly, with only what can be seen and asked.

In a study of 5.3 million adult emergency visits, the standard triage scale correctly flagged about two thirds of patients with high-acuity illness.

The longer the wait, the higher the risk.

Researchers followed 5.2 million admitted patients in England. After five hours in the emergency department, deaths within 30 days rose steadily with every additional hour, up to twelve.

England's emergency physicians estimate what that meant across 2023.

Some people give up and leave before being seen.

When waits stretch, some patients walk out before a doctor sees them. In Ontario, Canada, the monthly share leaving unseen topped its earlier high of 4.0% in 15 of the 36 months from April 2020, and outcomes after leaving got worse.
