## About / Overview (2,000 characters max)

> In the ER waiting room, it is hard to see who is getting worse.
>
> In 2022, US emergency departments handled 155 million visits. Many patients wait just to be triaged, and many wait again after triage, sometimes for hours. In one large US study, about 34 in 100 patients who turned out to need life-saving care were not flagged as high-acuity at triage. Meanwhile, hospitals have far fewer staffed beds than before the pandemic, and the nurses watching the room are stretched thin.
>
> None of this is for lack of effort. ER nurses, doctors and hospital leaders work hard inside a system running past its limits.
>
> PrIAge is working on ways to give ER nurses better information about patients while they wait. Nurses make the call.
>
> We're early. Right now we're talking with the people who do this work: ER nurses, ER doctors, hospital leaders, and anyone who has waited in an ER. Calls take 20 to 30 minutes. We won't ask about specific patients or your hospital, it isn't a sales call, and we'll share what we learn.
>
> We're four MBA students at UC Berkeley's Haas School of Business: a physician, a hospital software lead, a startup founder and a systems engineer.
>
> Talk with us: hello@priage.app
> The numbers and their sources: priage.app
